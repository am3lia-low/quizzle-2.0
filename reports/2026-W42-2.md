## Outcomes

### 💚 Jay Gatsby
*A dreamer with a plan, a mansion and a light across the bay.*

You believe in big hopes and grand gestures, and you're willing to reinvent yourself to reach them. Your optimism is magnetic: people turn up to whatever you've organised, even if they're not sure who you really are. Just remember the future is allowed to be different from the past.

Traits: hopeful, ambitious, generous

### 🌼 Daisy Buchanan
*Charming, breezy and impossible to forget.*

You light up a room without trying, and people lean in when you talk. You love beautiful things, lazy afternoons and anything that keeps the mood light. Underneath the sparkle you feel things deeply, even if you'd rather not make a fuss about it.

Traits: charming, graceful, spontaneous

### 📓 Nick Carraway
*The thoughtful observer everyone ends up confiding in.*

You'd rather watch the party than be its centre, and you notice everything. People trust you with their secrets because you listen and don't rush to judge. You're loyal, quietly funny and the one who remembers how the whole story really went.

Traits: observant, loyal, reflective

### ⛳ Jordan Baker
*Cool, confident and always a step ahead.*

You move through life with easy self-assurance and a dry sense of humour. You're independent, sporty and happiest when you're doing things your own way. Nobody rattles you, and you'd never wait around for someone else to make the plan.

Traits: confident, independent, witty

### 🦉 Owl Eyes
*The curious guest who wandered into the library and found the real story.*

While everyone else is dancing, you're checking whether the books on the shelves are real. You're delightfully curious, easily amazed and far more perceptive than people expect. A rare result: you're the one who sees through the show and still shows up when it counts.

Traits: curious, perceptive, sincere

## Agent notes

## Design choices

- **5 outcomes, winner scoring.** Gatsby, Daisy, Nick and Jordan are the four characters people know best, and they are distinct types, so "winner" fits better than "blend". Owl Eyes (the guest amazed that the library books are real) is the fifth, a rarer result (~15%) that rewards curiosity. Tom, Myrtle, George Wilson and Wolfsheim were left out on purpose: none of them would feel like a happy result, and Wolfsheim is written as an ethnic caricature.
- **9 questions.** A mix of scenarios (party arrival, a secret, running late), either/or picks (love, adventure or friendship) and preferences (afternoon, skill, home, compliment, night sound). Book nods stay light, so answers make sense to people who haven't read it.
- **Theme:** an Art Deco party night. Black-lacquer teal background with faint gold fan scallops, gold double-ruled card frames with a sunburst fan at the top, Poiret One display type and uppercase Josefin Sans. A picked answer fills with gold. Result cards take their outline from the outcome colour. The theme respects reduced motion.

## Critic notes

- **Pass 1 (blocking):**
  - Q8 "Spend the day at a museum on your own" bolted on a second idea and overlapped Q2. I replaced Q8 entirely with "You're running late for something important".
  - "Ask exactly how they found out" didn't fit a friend's own secret. It is now "Listen wide-eyed to every detail".
  - Also fixed from the non-blocking notes: the Jordan answer in Q1 sounded clingy, so it is now "Strike up a chat…". The car-engine sound in Q9 became "Distant laughter from a boat on the bay", which avoids the book's fatal crash.
- **Pass 2 (blocking):**
  - Q4 "Pick a colour" gave Gatsby away with "Green" (the green light) and Daisy with "White". It is now "Pick a skill you'd love to master".
  - The two Q8 "text ahead" answers overlapped. Daisy's answer is now "Breeze in as if nothing happened".
  - "Your voice" was a physical trait among character qualities. It is now "Your charm".
  - Honesty was tested three times, so Nick's compliment is now "Your patience" and Jordan's secret answer is "Tell them straight what you'd do".
- **Left as is:** "One great love" leans Gatsby; it's a fair, well-known nod in an either/or question. The "open window" and "lawn" motifs repeat. "Get distracted by something fascinating on the way" is a cause rather than a response, but it's charming.
