## Outcomes

### 🧠 The Frontier LLM
*A giant generalist who can chat about absolutely anything.*

Like the huge language models trained on mountains of text, you've soaked up a bit of everything and love connecting it all. You think out loud, find the right words fast and can switch from poetry to tax advice in one breath. Ask you a question and you'll give a thoughtful answer, plus three fun tangents.

Traits: knowledgeable, articulate, versatile

### 🔓 The Open-Source Model
*Shares everything, hides nothing, and gets better with friends.*

Open-source models publish their inner workings so anyone can study, tweak and improve them, and that's very you. You believe good ideas should be shared, you love a community project, and you're happiest when people take what you started and make it their own.

Traits: generous, transparent, collaborative

### 📱 The Tiny On-Device Model
*Small, quick and brilliant at doing more with less.*

Compact models run right on your phone: fast, private and efficient. You're the same. You don't need a data centre's worth of fuss to be clever. You get to the point, travel light and quietly get things done while everyone else is still loading.

Traits: efficient, focused, self-reliant

### 🌳 The Decision Tree
*Clear thinking, one sensible question at a time.*

A classic of traditional machine learning, a decision tree reaches answers through a chain of simple yes-or-no questions, and it can always explain exactly why it chose what it chose. That's you: logical, fair and refreshingly easy to follow. People trust your reasoning because you show your working.

Traits: logical, transparent, fair-minded

### 🎮 The Reinforcement Learner
*Tries, fails, learns, and eventually wins.*

Reinforcement learning agents learn by trial and error, chasing rewards until they master the game. You learn by doing, too. Mistakes don't scare you, because each one makes you sharper. Give you a goal and enough tries, and you'll find a way to beat it.

Traits: persistent, competitive, adaptable

### 🎨 The Diffusion Model
*Turns pure noise into something beautiful.*

Diffusion models start with random static and refine it, step by step, into a picture. You work the same way: you think in images and possibilities, you're comfortable with a messy first draft, and you have a gift for turning a vague idea into something people can actually see.

Traits: imaginative, visual, patient

### 🎯 The Recommender System
*Knows what you'll love before you do.*

Recommendation systems learn people's tastes and suggest the perfect next song, show or book. You're the friend with the uncanny gift for that. You notice what people light up about, you remember their favourites and your suggestions are basically never wrong.

Traits: perceptive, attentive, thoughtful

## Agent notes

## Design choices
- **Topic:** requested directly by the owner: which *kind of AI* your mind works like, revealed by how you think. It isn't in `topics.json`, so this PR leaves `topics.json` untouched.
- **Outcomes (7):** real families of AI, described accurately but playfully: The Frontier LLM, The Open-Source Model, The Tiny On-Device Model, The Decision Tree (traditional ML), The Reinforcement Learner, The Diffusion Model and The Recommender System. They're generic types, with no brands or specific products.
- **How it maps:** each answer reflects a thinking habit that mirrors how that kind of AI works. Thinking out loud and knowing a bit of everything → LLM. Sharing and improving others' ideas → open-source. Brevity and efficiency → on-device. Breaking problems into smaller questions → decision tree. Learning by trial and error → RL. Thinking in images and messy drafts → diffusion. Reading people's tastes → recommender.
- **9 questions, blend scoring:** people think in mixtures, and "62% Decision Tree, 25% Reinforcement Learner" is part of the fun.
- **Theme:** a friendly glowing circuit grid on deep navy with mint and lavender glows, Space Grotesk headings, a blinking terminal cursor after the title (off under reduced motion), and result cards that glow in each outcome's colour.

## Critic notes
- The first version used human thinking-style outcomes (Oracle, Analyst…). The owner clarified they wanted actual kinds of AI, so the quiz was rewritten. The earlier critic rounds on that version (reused questions, overlapping answers) informed this rewrite, and I checked every question text against all existing quizzes.
- **Round 1 on the rewrite (blocking):** "Learned by testing the limits" didn't answer "How do you like your rules?". **Fixed** → "Made to be tested". I also fixed several non-blocking notes: answers that echoed the Decision Tree description ("yes-or-no questions", "explain exactly why", "step by step"); "A tiny café corner" echoing "Tiny On-Device Model"; weak open-source and recommender links; the bag question, which was close to earlier packing questions (now a desk question); and the diffusion description, which wrongly implied all image models are diffusion models.
- **Round 2:** no blocking issues. I polished a run-on sentence in the RL description, replaced the awkward "A bit better every time you retell it" with "Testing what gets a laugh", and changed "humble" to "classic" in the intro.
- **Left as is:** the categories overlap in real life (an LLM can be open-source or run on a phone). That's fine for a fun quiz. Tiny-model answers are consistently about brevity, which is on purpose.
